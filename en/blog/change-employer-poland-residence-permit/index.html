For people already in Poland

Changing employer in Poland without losing your permit

Published and checked: 24 November 2026
General information, not legal advice. Mesh Consulting does not handle permits or immigration procedures. Each case depends on your type of permit and the date of your decision: always confirm with the voivodeship office or a lawyer. Last reviewed: 24 November 2026.

Changing jobs as a foreigner raises a fair question: will I lose my residence permit? The short answer is not automatically, but there are deadlines you must meet. Here is what the official Polish sources say.

1. The main rule: notify the voivode within 15 working days

If you live in Poland on a temporary residence and work permit (zezwolenie na pobyt czasowy i pracę, the “single permit”), you must notify in writing the voivode who issued it when you lose your job with any of the entities named in the permit. The deadline is 15 working days (not calendar days).

According to the voivodeship offices, this duty is also met if, within those 15 working days, you file an application to amend the permit (wniosek o zmianę zezwolenia).

2. What to do next

After notifying, the law provides two routes: apply to amend your current permit or apply for a new permit. According to Zielona Linia (the official information service of the employment services), an amendment costs PLN 220 versus PLN 440 for a new permit, and it does not change the permit’s expiry date.

Since 27 April 2026, applications for temporary, permanent and EU long-term resident permits are filed online only, through the MOS portal (mos.cudzoziemcy.gov.pl), signing in via login.gov.pl.

If you do nothing within the deadline, the voivode may start procedures to revoke the permit, because the purpose of your stay has ceased.

3. If you will work for a different employer

Official portals state that if you want to work for an employer other than the one named in your permit, you should apply for the amendment before starting. Working on conditions different from those in the permit can lead to its revocation and an obligation to return to your country. Usually the new employer must complete and sign annex no. 1 to the application.

4. When you do NOT need to amend the permit

The Act on Foreigners (art. 119) says no amendment or new permit is needed when, for example:

  • your employer’s seat, residence, name or legal form changes;
  • the workplace, or part of it, is taken over by another employer;
  • your working time increases with a proportional pay increase;
  • your job title changes without changing your duties;
  • a civil-law contract is replaced by an employment contract.

In those cases it is the employer who must notify the voivode, within 15 working days.

5. Common mistakes

  • Missing the 15-working-day deadline. This is the costliest mistake.
  • Starting at the new employer before the change is settled.
  • Relying on deadlines that are not in the law. The idea of “30 days to find another job” is widely repeated; we did not find it in the official sources we checked. The clear deadline is 15 working days to notify.

6. Check the date of your permit

Since 1 June 2025 a new act on the employment of foreigners applies, and some offices distinguish between permits issued before and after that date. Check with your voivodeship office which procedure applies to you.

7. Where to confirm your case

  • The foreigners’ department of the voivodeship office (in Wrocław: Dolnośląski Urząd Wojewódzki).
  • Zielona Linia: official information line, tel. 19524.
  • An immigration lawyer, if your situation is complex.
