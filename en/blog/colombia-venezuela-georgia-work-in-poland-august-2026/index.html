Colombia, Venezuela and Georgia: what changed for working in Poland

Published and checked: 24 November 2026
General information, not legal advice. Mesh Consulting does not handle permits or visas. Confirm your case with the Polish consulate or the voivodeship office. Last reviewed: 24 November 2026.

Since 22 August 2026 a regulation of the Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy applies to citizens of Colombia, Venezuela and Georgia. It is important information if you plan to come to work in Poland.

What the regulation says

  • It was published on 7 August 2026 and took effect on 22 August 2026.
  • Citizens of these three countries staying in Poland visa-free are not entitled to work under that regime, even if they hold a work permit or a declaration on entrusting work.

In practice: entering as a tourist and working on a work permit is no longer a valid route for these nationalities.

What route remains

According to Zielona Linia, to work legally you need:

  • a work visa obtained at the Polish consulate in your country, or a temporary residence permit (or another residence document) that allows work; and
  • where applicable, a work permit obtained by the employer.

Transitional rules

  • People who started working on a valid permit before 22 August 2026 may continue on the same terms until their visa-free stay ends.
  • If you applied for a residence permit, you may work while waiting for the decision only if you were entitled to work on the day you applied. Anyone who applied after 21 August 2026 does not have that right, except those covered by the transitional rule.

What to do if you are from one of these countries

  1. Do not travel to Poland as a tourist intending to work.
  2. Ask the Polish consulate in your country about work visa requirements.
  3. Ask whoever offers the job who will obtain the work permit and how long it takes.
  4. Be wary of anyone promising “fast” work without legal documents.

This information is indicative and is not a binding interpretation of the law.
